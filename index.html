Now that we know the elevation angle of the sun in the Google Earth map, we can measure the shadow lengths of each building one by one, and with that, we could get the heights of the buildings. But the problem is that, except for one building, every other building has unclear shadows. Going into Google Earth, I was able to get the 3D model of all the buildings, but I still struggled to find their heights. So here is what I have decided. I will calculate the height of the clearest building. And then I will make a very crude estimation of the heights of the other buildings relative to this clear one. And likewise, calculate the heights of all the buildings.

Then the next thing was to calculate the lengths and breadths of the buildings, which wasn't very difficult, and I was able to do that from Google Earth itself. With the elevation angle equal to e = 50.5 degrees (which is 0.88139 radians), and the length of the shadow l = 25.5 m from the image, the height of the building h1 = l * tan(e) = 30.933 m. And this will be used to estimate the heights of all the other buildings.

1.2 Modelling the Inflow Boundary Condition

2026-10-27 09:24:31

I would now like to get the direction and magnitude of wind in this region, because this will act as an inflow boundary condition, and is an absolutely necessary part of the solver. Thinking about the task, let me be very clear about what I want. There are three seasons in consideration: Summer, Winter, and Monsoon. And there are also different wind directions every day. Let me first see how much fluctuation we have in the magnitude and direction of winds before expressing my interest in how I want to capture the wind data after analysing it.

So, given below are the CSV files I got from the website: power.larc.nasa.gov. This was an amazing website. I have taken the direction and magnitude of winds at heights equal to 10 m and 50 m. Hourly data is taken for each from 1st Jan 2020 to 31st Dec 2025. And the files are provided here (2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025). With this data, I would first make an animation of the wind vector with time for all this time.

The CSV files given above have Wind Speeds at 10m altitude, Wind Speed at 50m altitude, Wind Direction at 10m altitude, and finally Wind Direction at 50m altitude. Wind travels at higher speeds at higher altitudes. And this is because, at greater altitudes, the friction experienced by the fluid due to the ground is lower. I will surely consider having a non-uniform speed profile in Z (height). And it goes by the Power Law, which I will discuss later.
